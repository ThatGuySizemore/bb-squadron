# Squadron watches; the Helper talks

Status: accepted. Supersedes the optional conversational front door and settings-only storage limits in [ADR-0006](0006-squadron-shows-and-starts.md), and clarifies idle lane state in [ADR-0008](0008-stops-are-states-not-rulings.md).

SQD-255 narrows the quiet Question rule below. A normal idle completion stays Flying, but elapsed time alone never creates Needs you. After five quiet minutes, the latest top-level reply in that completed turn must explicitly ask the owner: a direct question beginning Can/Could/Would/Will/Do/Are you, or a line beginning `Needs you:`. Quoted text and fenced code do not supply that evidence. Missing or unreadable question evidence creates no Question. Native pending requests and owner merge waits remain authoritative. When every remaining ticket has an open blocker outside the lane, the lane shows `Waiting on` and those issue identifiers, without notifying the Helper.

SQD-262 extends the marker to bold and mid-paragraph formatting; agents are instructed to put it at the start of its own line. Quoted text and inline code supply no question evidence. Negated status prose, empty formatted markers and `Needs you: none` or `Needs you: 0` stay quiet.

SQD-262 also gives the Lead `bb squadron stop '<SPEC-ID>' --reason two-strikes`. It sends a fixed policy wind-down message to the lane's own thread without claiming an owner action. Once its threads finish, the board reads that latest control input as Stopped with "Stopped after two strikes, wants a new go". The normal Needs you notifier wakes the project's Helper. A later Resume or other control request supersedes the old input; automatic system deliveries, including worker outcomes, do not. No reply prose, tracker comment or notification authorizes Resume, and Squadron stores no extra stop flag.

On 14 Nov 2026, SQD-196 and SQD-207 put observation in Squadron's code and conversation in an optional Helper thread. SQD-230 changes the original install-wide Helper to at most one Helper per BB project. Each Helper speaks for its own project only; there is no all-projects Helper or fallback. The Helper uses `bb squadron` to take the same actions as the board. It never polls. Lanes work without it. A notification grants no owner approval, including for an `accept:owner` merge.

The board watcher discovers every BB project and derives its Needs you even when no page is open. The Linear poller keeps those projects current, retaining its existing backoff. Other trackers keep their existing read caches. A project read failure produces a safe Setup line and does not clear earlier items it cannot check. Background observation does not fetch live agent steps.

New Needs you items enter a separate batch for their project and go only to that project's Helper. Each line names the project, kind, spec or ticket, one-line text and link in Owner words, using the same formatter as `bb squadron needs`. New arrivals move the quiet deadline to two minutes after the last arrival, capped at ten minutes after the first. Delivery uses the SDK's `queue-if-active` mode. A project without a Helper sends nothing and keeps Needs you on its board; its undelivered batch waits and retries when a Helper is available. A failed send remains pending.

Per-project plugin storage retains reported item keys, pending lines and deadlines, and the last observed landing state per lane solely for delivery. Reported keys clear when a successful observation establishes that the item cleared, so a returning item is new. A failed read cannot establish a clear. Pending items that clear before delivery are removed. Successful queued sends count as delivered. A restart retains the batch deadline, rechecks pending Needs you before delivery, and does not replay reported items. The SDK send and storage write are separate operations; this does not claim transactional exactly-once delivery across a process crash between them.

A lane observed underway that later has a merged PR or a Landed spec adds one Landed line to the same batch. Landed is an event, not Needs you. Initial historical landings form a baseline and do not wake the Helper. A reported landing never repeats. Ticket moves, PR opening or readiness, and checks do not wake it.

An idle Lead's latest `turn/completed` event with status `completed` supplies the quiet clock through its event-row `createdAt`, in epoch milliseconds. A generic thread `updatedAt` is not an idle timestamp. SDK interruptions, failed turns, the board or CLI's fixed Stop request, and missing or unreadable lifecycle evidence retain Stopped. A normal completed turn stays Flying, and after five idle minutes with no running workers or background work, it adds a Question, "The Lead is waiting on you", linking the Lead. Landed lanes and owner merge waits never receive that quiet Question. The board and notification use the same derivation. No assistant prose is interpreted as an approval or a stop ruling.

SQD-207 provides observation and notification delivery. SQD-211 shares the board observation callback and one-shot board reads for its daily summary, without another tracker poller. Tests drive the real plugin through the SDK fake host and fake timers, including unattended discovery, batching, reload, clears, landings and quiet-Lead exclusions. Live BB delivery remains a separate integration check.

The daily summary registers one stable minute cron schedule. The SDK registers schedules only at load and interprets them in server-local time. Each invocation reads the current Daily summary time setting, default `08:00`, accepting `HH:MM` or `Off`. It sends at or after that time once per local calendar day. This catches up after downtime or a skipped clock hour and suppresses a repeated hour or a second send after a time change. The clock is the BB server clock, independent of the browser timezone. Only code checks the clock; no agent monitors it.

Each project's summary queues one message to its own Helper with only that project's Landed since last summary, Flying and Needs you count. Summary delivery history and boundaries are separate per project. A project without a Helper sends nothing. All underway lanes count as Flying, including lanes in review or waiting on the owner. The first summary includes observed Landed lanes. The shared watcher remembers landings even while summaries are Off or no Helper exists, so a lane disappearing before the next summary does not erase it. Storage retains landing identities, undelivered landings and the last successful summary boundary and local day across reload. A failed send leaves history pending for the next schedule check; a successful queued send advances the boundary. As with notifications, send and storage are separate operations, with no exactly-once claim across a process crash between them. Tests invoke the registered schedule through the fake host, including time changes, reload, missing Helper, failed sends and server-local DST.

Helper selection is stored by BB project ID. On upgrade, the former install-wide Helper is kept for the project its thread belongs to; other projects start without one. The board's Helper controls apply only to the selected project. `bb squadron helper use` uses the calling thread's project, with `--project` available explicitly. A thread can be Helper for only one project at a time; assigning it to another project moves it.

`bb squadron status` and `needs` default to the calling thread's project. `--project` selects another by exact ID or unique name, and `--all` lists every project for the owner. With no calling project, the command requires one of those selectors. Combining them is an error. The bundled Helper skill uses its project by default.

SQD-264 gives the Lead `bb squadron wait <SPEC-ID> --on <lane or reason>` for a wait Linear doesn't record, such as two lanes needing the same files. It sends a fixed message that names the target, and the board reads it as a declared wait only while no later Lead turn has started. The lane shows "Waiting on <target>", isn't Stopped, isn't under Needs you and doesn't wake the Helper. A wait naming an issue clears when that issue closes, including when the tracker reader has already dropped it; an owner question in the Lead's reply still reaches Needs you; any other reason clears when the Lead runs again. As with two strikes, no reply prose is classified.

SQD-270 adds `--reason time-limit` ("Stopped at its 8-hour limit. Work saved. Resume to continue.") and words a plain Stop as "The lane was stopped. Resume to continue." A declared wait survives a message from another thread that the Lead only answers: the board looks past such a message for the wait, and the wait clears only when the Lead takes a turn of its own, launches a child, or the named lane lands. The simpler of the two options in the ticket was a board-side read, so the Lead's skill needs no "declare it again" step.

SQD-275: a Lead reads Stopped only on recorded evidence: a Stop control (plain, two strikes or time limit), an interrupted or failed turn, a failed thread, or no history at all. A message from another thread that the Lead only answers keeps a recorded Stop, as it keeps a wait; the Stop's own wind-down (its turn, saved files, worker outcomes) isn't the Lead running again, and a turn of the Lead's own after the Stop is. A history Squadron can't read is no evidence of a Stop: the lane stays Flying with a Needs you line "Squadron couldn't read the Lead for <ID>. Open it to check." Replaying the real WW-67 and WW-769 histories through the reader gave Waiting and a quiet idle Lead, so the cause of their "The lane stopped." wasn't in the event rules; the read at 20:27Z most likely failed or timed out on those long histories, which this change now surfaces instead of hiding.

The unread line carries the reason: a read error's message, or "no finished turn is recorded" when the Lead has history but no turn boundary. A thread with no history at all still reads Stopped. Live, on 15 Nov 2026, SQD-273 and SQD-275 read Stopped after declaring a wait, while replaying their recorded histories through the same reader returned the wait. The deployed bundle's reader matches this source, so the difference lies in what the live reader returned. Squadron now says which, instead of guessing Stopped.
